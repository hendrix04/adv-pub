#lore #UnifiedChurch
The Unified Church grew out of a late DR1400's movement that respected the power of all the Gods. Its growth was often a matter of practicality. For instance, nominally Apolloite (aka Lathanderan) sailors were already giving Thor (aka Talos) offerings when they went to sea to ward off his wrath. It was a simple extension of the concept to include offerings to other major deities to avoid the pitfalls or gain the favors of their portfolio. People began relocating spiders instead of smashing them as a small gesture to Lloth; even good wizards would invoke the name Vecna as they sought to unlock new secrets of the multiverse; Dwarven warriors asked Gruumush for endurance and cunning in battle while Orc smiths sought Moradin's blessings on their forges. 

A formal Unified Church was consecrated in Waterdeep in 1505 on the site of the Cathedral of the Dawnfather, and it soon spread across the sword coast, though there are still many stubborn holdouts who still offer their worship to but a single God.

Theologists have spent years reconsidering the wider state of divinity in the Realms and when the Unified Church was consecrated they pronounced all the Gods who were not listed in the Pantheon as Celestial or Demonic beings. They pointed to Demon Lords like Orcus and Yeenoghu, who are often seen as 'God like' and said that lesser Gods like Eilistralee had commensurate power and must be considered equivalent Angelic beings. In the Church's view such beings are still worthy of worship, and potentially could find their way into the Pantheon to replace one of the existing Gods.

The Church acknowledges the power of these lesser divine creatures, and its Dogma does not prohibit their veneration. However, the Church does insist that the official Pantheon be respected by all, and supplicants to individual powers in official positions must acknowledge the primacy of the Pantheon.

Elturgard was one of the first Nation-States to wholly adopt the Dogma of the Unified Church. This surprised some observers, as the city had a long history of being fanatical followers of Apollo (nee Lathander). However, the city's captivity in the Hells and the deceit of Thevius Kreig had shown the dangers of that route. High Overseer Pherria Jynks was one of the Theologans who developed the Dogma of the Unified Church. She felt that recognition of the role Asmodeus and the Hells played in the cosmological eco-system would have prevented the Fall of Elturel. "Balance is important. Failure to maintain balance can lead to catastrophe as the universe *will* eventually reset itself. The Druids have always warned us about straying too far in any direction. Elturel paid the price for not heeding their warnings."

To expound on this idea:

*However, the Church does insist that the official Pantheon be respected by all, and supplicants to individual powers in official positions must acknowledge the primacy of the Pantheon.*

e.g. Caine asks Eilistralee to bless a midnight dance festival in Kheldell, but he does so "In the name of all the Gods of the Realms" or a Cleric in Calimport might beseech Yeenoghu not to sate his hunger in the desert, but to feed his people elsewhere, "as the Gods have intended."

The Unified Church was faced with a Theological conundrum when it went about trying to organize its worship practices. Hundreds of Gods were known to exist in the realms. And it has also been shown that the number of Gods is not fixed, that they can die, that mortals can ascend in times of upheaval, and certain powerful entities can ascend in more mundane eras. Should the church acknowledge all beings who can grant divine powers to mortals as Gods? The number of worshipers was a factor in the power of the Gods, but was not the defining characteristic. Nor was it a God's history in the multiverse. Asmodeus and Lolth both ascended from their status as lords of lower planes to godhood. Despite not being Gods at the beginning they are certainly now among the most powerful of the Pantheon. What about Vestiges? The husks of dead Gods still retain divine powers that could be tapped into by some, and with enough effort those Vestiges could be reborn as living Gods again. 

It was eventually decided that the Pantheon would be the only entities who are referred to as the Gods of the Realms, and that any divine creatures who were not included in the Pantheon were to be regarded as Celestial or Fiendish creatures, Angels and Demons, rather than being labeled as 'lesser', 'minor', or 'demi-' Gods. The nomenclature could seem arbitrary, but it was a deliberate construct to create the broadest of lines between the Pantheon, which presented a perfect Divine balance in the world, and the potentially infinite number of other divine beings that could exist.

As one theologian commented, "Here are the Divine Lords. Unlike in Waterdeep, none are masked. Each has an equal power in the universe, and each is in perfect balance with the others. They rule collectively, and while each may strive for a larger influence, their grasping will be prevented by an equal and opposite grasp from their peers. Lawful stability will be preserved, but chaos leading to innovation and growth will likewise remain. Neutral balance will always return, no matter how the Divine Lords try to outmaneuver each other."
