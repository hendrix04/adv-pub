#Factions #16th-century
After the events known as the *Rise of Tiamat* in the early 1490s, the organized factions of the realms found themselves exhausted financially and otherwise and their influence waned for the last decade of the 15th century through the first decade of the 16th. With the increasing power of the Pantheon the gods used their influence to reform the old factions or create new ones to serve as their mortal agents in the world. The point seems to have been to maintain the détente between the members of the Pantheon, rather than expand influence. By 1515 there are 16 factions of various sizes and levels of influence overtly sponsored by individual members of the Pantheon plus another 5 factions of significant stature that have no official ties to the Pantheon, but are linked to non-mortal entities. 

The Matron of Ravens has no factions. Speculation holds that the Matron did not need a mortal faction since as the Keeper of Fates she knows what will come to pass and need not interfere.

**The Stone League** is essentially a defensive pact among the Dwarven city-states of the north. It also enjoys limited support among the cities of the Silver Marches, as well as promoting the Dwarven  expatriate community across the north.

**Captain's Call** is an informal association of seafarers on the Sea of Swords, including some who fight piracy and those who may practice it from time to time. It has no formal structure, and in general acts as a mutual aid society.

**Cult of the Dragon** is a rump organization from the Cult's heyday, instead of seeking to bring Tiamat to the world, it now promotes greater understanding of the views of Chromatic Dragons and has a educational arm to teach humanoids "how to not look like food" to dragons. The Cult teaches that if humanoids only understood their place in the world then dragons would not be so inclined to punish their impudence, and everyone would live a lot longer. Also don't cry if a dragon eats your cow. Consider it a blessing. There are not too many humanoids who have fully bought in to this new philosophy as of yet.

**Society of Friends** Yolonda's cult spreads the Gospel of Friendship, Plenty, and Good food across Faerun. 

The **Shadow Thieves** are a venerable organization, albeit not a well known one, preferring to work in the background to achieve their aims, which are generally to keep other forces from consolidating power.

**House Baenre ** is the ruling house of Menzoberranzan, and thus the largest promotor of Lolth in Faerun. After centuries of intrigue, the Spider Queen's chosen House has essentially played to a draw against all their enemies, real or imagined.

**The Pike and Square** is a fraternal organization. Gruumush, as God of War, has brought together war leaders of diverse races and nations to better promote the means to wage and win wars, wherever they may occur. Orc, Elf, Dwarf; Luskan, Baldurian, Islander all meet as peers to discuss ways to expand their share of their various Crown's spending and to learn about new war material to spend it on.

**The Fraternity of Scribes** publicly is less Fraternity and more book club. The Fraternity ensures the rapid diffusion of new knowledge across the continent. Its members can be found as courtiers in every throne room in Faerun, and in every college as well. Less commonly known, is the Monks trained in Candlekeep and elsewhere are also sworn to maintain the balance among the members of the new Pantheon. They may secretly support one faction one day, and oppose them the next as the tides of fate ebb and flow.

The **Dragon's Guard** was a more pragmatic successor organization to the **Order of the Guantlet**. The later had been weakened by the fight with the Dragon Cult, but utterly devastated by the Fall of Elturel. Guided by Bahamut, the **Guard** sought to focus the strength of existing holy/military orders where they could do the most good, rather than becoming 'just another gang of paladins'. **Duke Tobias Wavesilver**, ruler of Leilon, gave generously to the group in the early 1500s and rose to a position of unquestioned respect, and was recently drafted to become leader of the organization by a grass-roots movement from the rank and file.

**The Emerald Enclave** suffered the most organizational loss after the fight with the Dragon Cult, effectively ceasing to be in the north. Its power center in the Vilhon Reach was less disrupted, and by the early 1500s they had reestablished their cells in the large forests of the Sword Coast. Still much stronger in the south, they depended upon 'soft power' to achieve their aims in the north.

The **Merchant's Guild** is an organization of business interests of the Sword Coast. Commerce, being the life blood of nations, is an essential facet of society, but one that found itself underrepresented at the start of the 16th century withThe Lord's Alliance focused on matters millitary, and the Zhentarim both diminished in power and representing only their own limited commercial interests. A new organization was established by leading merchants across the sword coast to advance their common interests. A wholly democratic organization, each member has but one vote in elections for leadership, no matter how large their own personal fortune. This has led to more modest merchants having control of the group. Its current leader is **Silas Greenback**, who is also the **Water Baron of Yartar.** He appears to be avoiding any conflict of interest well and his leadership is well regarded.

The **Harpers** are a storied organization with ties to some of the most remarkable individuals the Realms have ever known. Currently lead by **Laeral Silverhand** who is the Open Lord of Waterdeep, as well as a Chosen of the Gods and a Daughter of the Goddess of magic, the Harpers have always acted clandestinely, supporting low level operatives in attempts to disrupt the schemes of "Evil" actors across the Realms. The Harpers may never be as powerful as they were in the days when Silverhand's husband **the Blackstaff, Khelban Arunsun** led the organization, but they still have their finger on the pulse of the realms, and agents seemingly everywhere.

The **Red Wizards** of Thay are a nation of Undead Wizards. Thay has been trying to expand its influence in the North for the last 150 years, with limited success. An oppressive nation to the living, there are many Thayan refugees living in the north, as well as loyal Thayan Wizards  just doing magical archeology and not trying to conquer the land to be found nearly anywhere. The Red Wizards are often an unsubtle force, which makes them an odd choice for "The Whispered One" to have selected as his mortal representatives in the Realms.

The **Zhentarim** or commonly "Zhents" are an organization of Mercenaries and merchants. Over their history they have been hijacked to be the agents of the evil gods Bane and Cyric, as well as become the personal army of the Wizard Manshoon. Perhaps no organization in the Realms has had such wild swings in terms of success and failure. The Zhents traditional strongholds in the Moonsea have been diminished or lost outright. Their return to the Sword Coast coincided with the patronage of Asmodeus, and many open adherents of Asmodeus joined the group. The Zhents now appear as a legitimate organization, operating in the public eye. Their caravans are statistically safer than those of other groups, allowing them to charge a premium. Their mercenary forces have recently been used by a number of high profile private interests and even some state actors with good results. They **Black Network** still retains its sinister reputation, however they do not appear to be bothered by it, as it seems to be one factor in the safety of their caravan trade. **Manshoon** remains the leader of the organization, and a truce between the three known Clones of Manshoon has led the group to be divided into three franchises divided geographically. The Manshoon in Waterdeep oversees the Network's activities in the North.


The **Lords' Alliance** is a military alliance among the larger cities of the Sword Coast. It dates back to 1325, and in turn has united the city-states against threats from Amn, Luskan, Many-Arrows, Piracy, Northman raiders, the Zhentarim, and other threats. Nearly defunct in the 1490's as local leaders succumed to isolationism, it was rejuvenated in the 1510's under the leadership of Neverwinter's Lord Dagult Neverember, focused solely on mutual military assistance, leaving cultural and trade agreements for others to concern themselves with. Notably the Duchy of Leilon and its vassals are *not* signatories to the Alliance, but send representatives to the **Council of Lords**, the regularly scheduled meeting of the Alliance, and also contribute to the upkeep of **Revel's End**, a notorious prison in Frozenfar operated by the Alliance.

**The Unified Church of the Realms** has been detailed elsewhere. But it is worth mentioning that it is now the most widespread and influential organization in Faerun. Even in Mezzoberrazan where Lolth worship is still the state religion, the UCR has made inroads, and has tacit approval from the ruling council of the city to operate temples. Notably the symbol of the Pantheon used there puts Lolth at the top and connects the other gods to her with webs, implying Lolth's supremacy or at least leadership. This may explain why the new church made such a rapid advance everywhere. Allowing the local or favorite God to appear to be in control of the Pantheon makes the Pantheon easy to adopt, "Oh, look at all the servants/allies my God has", is a reassuring idea.

**The Unreformed Church of Love** is the Cult of Sune. Of all the reclassified gods, it is the followers of **Sune Firehair** who found themselves most adrift without an adequate replacement. **Corellon** absorbed her portfolio, but Corellon's affect lacked a certain *je ne sai quoi* that the Sunites found wanting. He had beauty certainly, but her passion was absent. 
The **UCL** seems named to deliberately mock the **UCR**, and Sunites continue to practice their worship in Her name alone, in overt defiance of the UCR's dogma. For Sune's part, perhaps she enjoys the demonstrations, as she continues to anoint those who please her with her traditional blessings.

The **Cult of Asmodeus** continues to exist in small cells around the Sword Coast. Its current high priest is a Bard known as Chuckavin. Oddly, their worship practices have morphed significantly since the Cassalanters were deposed from their position of high priests. The Cult is now even more "culty", and centered on worshiping *with* the high priest as the only path to personal communion with the God. Children of these cultists are frequently Tieflings of Ruavinic lineage.

The **Circle of Tears** are followers of the Moon Goddess **Selune**, while Apollo is God of both Sun and Moon, some Selunese found transitioning difficult. They now say their goddess is imprisoned in one of the Tears of Selune, and focus their prayers on them in hopes of freeing her one day.

**Shar**, goddess of night and darkness, is also held to be imprisoned in the same cell as her sister, much to Shar's dismay. The **Dark Veil** wishes to both free their goddess and murder her sister, goals that at first glance seem to be in conflict with one another.


**The Church of Eilistraee**, has always been a small but devout sect. With the resurrection of their Goddess only a few years ago, and many of the faithful having actually seen her dancing, giving her up so soon has been difficult without a fully adequate replacement. Many of the Dark Dancer's faithful continue their practice unconcerned with the opinion of others.

**Other** military orders and thieves' guilds exist and could be considered factions, especially locally. Some of which have attracted divine interest from the Pantheon and other non-mortal entities, some of which serve purely mortal masters. Examples include **Force Grey** in Waterdeep, **The Flamisng Fist** of Baldur's Gate,  **The Xanathar Guild** in Waterdeep, and **Bregan d'Aerthe** in Luskan and elsewhere.
