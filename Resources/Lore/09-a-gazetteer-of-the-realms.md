A(n abridged) Gazetteer of The Realms:

**Icewind Dale**’s largest settlement, **Bryn Shander** was the biggest of the **Ten Towns** (a loose confederation of frontier villages) and was known for being a trading center. It was the gateway to its fellow towns of the dale, as well as their lifeline to the rest of civilization to the south.

“What's Bryn Shander like, ye ask? It's sixty score humans, packed together like fish in a barrel an' smelling about as sweet. Its roads are paved with the muck o' the cart horses ye're constantly rubbing shoulders with, an' its alehouses are filled with scoundrels that no other city in Faerûn would have. An' after three weeks on Ten Trail. you'll swear there was no lovelier sight!”
— Beorne Steelstrike 

Government in the Dale had a *laissez-faire* approach. Official decisions for the towns were made by a council consisting of one influential member from each town. These council members, called speakers, met in the council building in Bryn Shander, which was unanimously recognized as the capital of the Ten Towns, and of Icewind Dale itself, once a month during the summer season, and once every three in the winter.

Economically, the Dale relied exclusively on the fishing and sale of knucklehead trout, along with the scrimshaw made from its bones. This industry allowed the Ten Towns to remain in existence. Each of the lakeside settlements maintained their own fleet of fishing ships.

Demographics skewed human, and although there were Dwarven Halls beneath the Dale there was little commerce between the two societies. Inhabitants were hardy survivors as the environment was harsh and unforgiving. Many were petty criminals fleeing from justice, who found their continued freedom demanded a lifetime of hard labor.

**Luskan** also known as the City of Sails, was a port city at the mouth of the River Mirar on the Sword Coast North. Luskan, despite the presence of the Ten Towns, was considered by many to be civilization's farthest reach.

Although once a bustling city, in the days prior to the Spellplague, the city would suffer greatly at the hands of Arklem Greeth. The resulting disaster would allow the Spellplague to nearly annihilate the city, with it having only a fraction of its former populace.

“Tucked like a gold filling (or some would say a pus-filled abscess) at the mouth of the River Mirar, Luskan is a city of merchant princesses, swarthy bandit princes, and the most diverse, cutthroat, and fractured cabal of pirates on Toril. Torn into factions, these piratical groups are known as "Ships."”
— Excerpt from Corsairs of Luskan

Government had traditionally been an Oligarchy of Pirate High Captains, with five ruling "Ships" making and enforcing laws. A lich named Arklem Greeth rose to power in the Arcane Brotherhood, a cabal of Wizards who controlled the Ships for a time, his fall, and the spellplague devastated the city. In 1480 there was no functional government in the city at all, but shortly after the five High Captains began legitimate trading networks, and a semblance of order was restored. Economically, in the 16th century the city has continued its growth in trade, and Luskan ships sail to every port on the sword coast and even further afield, loaded with goods most often from Mirabar, but also Icewind Dale, and even as far as Silverymoon.

**Neverwinter**, also known as the City of Skilled Hands and the Jewel of the North, was a bustling, cultured, and cosmopolitan city-state in northwest Faerûn. Neverwinter was regarded by Volo as the most cosmopolitan and civilized city in all of Faerûn. The city was a member in good standing of the Lords' Alliance. Known for its craftsfolk and gardeners, the city's multi-colored-glass lamps, precision water clocks, exquisite jewelry, and magnificent gardens ensured the warm winters were colorful and the summers were rich with fresh fruit.

“Neverwinter is a friendly city of craftsmen, who trade extensively via the great merchants of Waterdeep; their water-clocks and multi-hued lamps can be found throughout the Realms. Neverwinter gained its name from the skill of its gardeners, who contrived to keep flowers blooming throughout the months of snow—a practice they continue with pride.”
— Elminster's notes on Neverwinter

Despite weathering the Spellplague better than many cities, in 1451 the city was destroyed, and its Royal line extinguished. The long-term power vacuum allowed the Open Lord of Waterdeep, **Dagult Neverember**, to concoct a scheme to take control over the city, which he accomplished incrementally over a number of years. Neverember succeeded in rebuilding Neverwinter,, though his methods were not without controversy, and neither were his actions in Waterdeep. Neverember lost his position as Open Lord and faced accusations of corruption and embezzlement that were never proven. He retains his position as Lord of Neverwinter and rules semi-despotically, using taxation and regulation to keep nobles and guilds from organizing and amassing power which could be used against him.

Economically the city has become wealthy rebuilding itself. Without guilds to restrict trade or construction, those who wanted to start a business in Neverwinter could simply do so, and those traders who dealt with basic products, such as foodstuffs, became wealthy just by selling their goods in the city. Likewise, there was demand for many jobs, and those who offered their services either as workers or as apprentices had plenty of options despite the high competition.

Neverember once had dreams of Empire and sought to expand Neverwinter through economic dominance of its neighbors. His Imperial dreams were largely quashed by the unlikely rise of its once ruined and unassuming neighbor, Leilon.

**Leilon**, or more formally **The Grand Duchy of Leilon** is an unlikely power on the Sword Coast. The town itself had only 3000 residents at its height in the 14th century, and was abandoned entirely during the Spellplague. Neverember sponsored a rebuilding effort when he was Open Lord of Waterdeep, which was taken over by a Waterdavian noble named Tobias Wavesilver, who bought up many defunct Noble claims to lands between Waterdeep and Neverwinter, including the lordship of Leilon. Another noble family, the Margasters were also buying up lapsed titles, and tensions between the two houses led to a Civil War, charges of Treason, and a demonic plot against the city being unwound by adventurers. With Wavesilver coming out on top of the war with the Margasters, he established a Dutchy in Leilon, and secured a number of Vassals to protect his new lands including pirates, dwarves, an adventuring party, and an ancient green dragon!

Wavesilver, self-crowned Duke in 1492, has been a benign ruler for over 20 years. Taxes are low and regulations loose, and trouble does not often visit his lands, probably due to his deft political maneuvers. His vassals rule their fiefdoms as they see fit, largely as benignly as Wavesilver himself. The town sill is little more than a hamlet, its largest economic engine being the Ducal estate. Wavesilver is active in Sword Coast politics and defense, though Leilon is only an observer to the Lord's Alliance and not a full member. He also leads a Divine faction known to advance Bahamut's interests. His enemies accuse him of piracy and devil worship, as his family fortune was made through merchant shipping, and his Liegedom over a green dragon stirred rumors of infernal bargains.
