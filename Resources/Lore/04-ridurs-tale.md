#Ridur

**Ridur's Tale** 

*Castle Swordmount, Fall 1510*

*Tap-tap-tap-tap-tap went the dragon's back claws on the stone flooring, its echo smothered in the rich tapestries on the walls. The young gold dragon was restless, his body involuntarily fidgeting as he pored over a thick, grey fabric-covered tome opened on the oak lectern  before him.* 

“I really don't want to read 'Mortal Godhood in the Time of Troubles: A Dialectic analysis of Divine Ascension'. It is worse than 'A History of Netheril and its Impact on Faerun's Economy, Trade, and Diplomacy in the 16th century and beyond'” *Aura whined.* “Its boring. Its worse than when Uncle Ruavin and Uncle Bethel start talking in maths or want to do something tedious. And Uncle Bethel likes watching paint dry. He made me sit with him one day and just watch a freshly whitewashed fence dry for like a whole hour. He was excited by it.” *The young gold dragon then rolled his eyes, which would have caused some distress to anyone who saw it, had the only creatures in the room with him not already been clenching their eyes closed and holding their ears to blot out the sound of the whining. The whining of a Gold Dragon is one of nature's rarest events, and a remarkably unpleasant one. Castle Swordmount was well built, and soundproofing had been part of the design, so while the dragon's words stopped at he door of his chambers, the negative energy radiated throughout the structure, causing more than one mere humanoid to suffer a painful bout of nausea.* “I could write a better history and I've never been anywhere, or met anyone, or done anything or seen something beyond what you can see from the top of this tower!”

*His tutor was **Kosi,** who appeared as a venerable gold elf, but was in fact a Ki-Rin, a celestial servant of good Gods who had been summoned by **Ruavin Thrice-chosen**, a friend and counselor of Aura's adoptive father, **Caine of Swordmount.** The ancient-looking gold elf sighed,* “Please remember your tone, young Lord. Your people are not built to withstand your wrath.” *It was always hard to tell if Kosi was a master of sarcasm or if they truly lacked a sense of humor entirely. Aura sulked a moment, but stopped whining as he went on.* “I just can't stand reading another one of these dreadfully written histories. This is an amazing tale, or it would be if it were not ruined by the author's complete lack of verve. Let me read **Volo**'s version. I don't care if half of it is made up. At least it can't be used as a component for the sleep spell.” 

“We've talked about Volo before. None of that scribe's works are a regular part of your curricula because they are unreliable. I understand they have some entertainment value, but they are severely lacking in educational capacity.”

“Phhhhhbbbbbt!” 


*Across the room a dark skinned Halfling with a bad complexion, blew a juicy raspberry at the Elf. **'Spit'** was actually an Imp, another servant of Ruavin's recruited for Aura's education.* “Nonsense. Volo is a great writer. And objectively a curriculum that doesn't include the opportunity to learn how to glean truth buried in fable, fiction, and lie is useless in this world. Pull your head out of Mt. Celestia's clouds and come back down here where the real people live. Kid, go ahead and do your thing to that bundle of outhouse wipes. It has no useful application for anyone, ever. I'm sure if we leave it open it will cause a drought and all the crops will fail.” *his tone suddenly changes from snide to excitement* “Oh, oh, oh! wait, don't burn it! Lets go bury it in a wheatfield and turn this place into a desert. Heh. That would be awesome!”, *the halfling began a deep, rolling, belly laugh.*


*Aura had smiled when Spit told him to 'do your thing' and had begun to inhale a bit in preparation for producing his fire breath. But the laughter snapped him out of his funk.* “You're right. This is a powerful piece of work. And I suppose I need to read it. I can appreciate the knowledge, but I can also despise its poor quality. We need important works to be better written. This history deserves to be be told in a way that people will want to learn it.” 

“Well, if the young Lord wishes to have these lessons written in a more suitable style, I can only suggest that when you master the material you might be inclined to rewrite it in a more accessible tone. Volo's version of the events are suitable as an adjunct text, once the primary source is fully examined.” *Aura's eyes rolled again, and Spit snickered, but Kosi continued on,* “I agree that the scribe Awlinson lacks an engaging style, however as my esteemed counterpart Mr. Saliva almost managed to point out, your ability to glean information from flawed sources is an essential skill. Finish this and then we can examine ”Volo's Guide to the Time of Troubles”.


*Later, in Caine, Lord Swordmount's private office, the two tutors met with the Lord of the land and his cadre of advisors; **Castillian Anispara Wildhunt, Captain of the Guard Gondrek Giantsbane, Court Wizard Conde Ruavin Ea,** and **Court Architect Doctor Bethel of Ammarindar.** The old elf began* “Your heir is a bright student, but lacks patience. A Dragon raised with its brood mates learns history at its parents' wing, usually in flight on the way to see the place where it happened. I'd prefer to do the same with Aura, but our current forms are not conducive to such excursions, neither in flying ability nor in defensive capabilities. Even in our true forms we would honestly be lacking. A young gold dragon escorted by a Ki-Rin and an Imp would soon be targeted by hostile Giants, Chromatic Dragons, and illiterate adventurers. None of which would be completely safe to engage, or completely certain to evade.” *The halfling interrupted,* “What my long winded associate is getting at is Aura can't sit still long enough to read a book. He isn't built for it. In fifty or a hundred years he'll grow some patience, but for now he needs to roam. And we aren't the right sort of people to go with him.”


“Exactly what are you proposing?” *Caine's deep bass voice could be felt by everyone in the chamber, but his tone was not unkind or impatient. The Barbarian wore  the Crown of Swordmount, which was much like a Headband of Intellect. His long use of such items had steadily improved his natural brain function, and while he no longer felt quite as foggy minded without it, the great Dragonborn Noble knew when it came to making important decisions wearing the Crown was as essential to an optimal outcome as seeking advice from his counselors.*
*Kosi said,* “We can't give Aura the upbringing of his kind, but we might be able to do one better. At his age you were already a successful adventurer, and had earned a Noble title by feat of arms. He can't see the world a dragon, but he could as a Dragonborn. A few years on the road as an adventurer will give him the perspective and experience his education needs and that we can't deliver.” 
*Anispara objected,* “But young gold dragons can't reliably shapechange. Aura has leaned the art a bit from **Merriweather Wavesilver,** but he can only hold the form for maybe a minute at best.”


*Kosi tried to look sheepish, an expression that no one had ever seen the tutor attempt to make before. It was not entirely successful and ended up more akin to digestive discomfort, much to the amusement of everyone, the halfling especially. Spit explained,* “Well, we know that he's not ready for that kind of change. But Ruavin is here. For a change,” *he mutters under his breath, just loud enough so that no one could miss it,* “and he could do to Aura what he did to us. True Polymorph isn't going to do the trick, because the first time anyone pops off a Dispel Magic, he's back to being a dragon, and that would be embarrassing at best. But that Divine Polymorph twist Old Smoky Eyes used on us can work on non-celestials too, and then Aura really will be an elf, or a goblin, or whatever form he feels like he wants to try on, until Ruavin undoes the spell with his Gods-given hocus-pocusing.”

*Before Ruavin could interject, Bethel started to giggle.* “Old Smokey Eyes. That's hilarious. Because the hell-fire eyeball thing. And also the pretty Elf-face thing. He he. But Mr. Saliva is right, you could totally turn Aura into a non-dispelable humanoid.” *And then the two Aracanists were talking in maths again discussing how to implement the necessary changes to the spell so that it would affect creatures from the material plane, and everyone in the room knew what was being proposed was feasible, and then the discussion as to whether it was a good idea or not really began.*
